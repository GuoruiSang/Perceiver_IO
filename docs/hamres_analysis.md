# HamRes Explosion Analysis (2DoF)

HamRes measures violation of Hamilton's equations: `HamRes = mean(r_q²)/Var(dq) + mean(r_p²)/Var(dp)`, where `r_q = dq/dt - ∂H/∂p` and `r_p = dp/dt - (-∂H/∂q + τ)`. The derivatives `dq/dt`, `dp/dt` are estimated via central finite differences with `dt = 0.0002`. The normalization constants from HNN training are `Var(dq) = 0.479` and `Var(dp) = 0.368`.

The dominant term is `mse_p/Var(dp)`. For a typical unguided trajectory, the finite-differenced `dp/dt` has magnitude ~11.4, while the Hamiltonian target `-∂H/∂q + τ` is only ~0.19 — a ~60× scale mismatch. This is because DPF-generated momentum contains small inter-step noise (`|Δp| ≈ 0.005`) that gets amplified ~2500× by the `÷2dt` operation. As a result, the residual `r_p ≈ dp/dt` and `mse_p ≈ 235`, yielding a per-sample HamRes of ~645. Across 1000 samples (GP, L=1000), the median is 405, P95 is 2086, and P99 reaches 5094 — a heavy-tailed distribution where the mean (2236) is 5.5× the median. The plot used 100-sample data with mean±std bands; with so few samples, a single extreme outlier (estimated ~2.8M) inflates the mean to ~28,857 and std to ~275,046, producing the ~300K visual spikes. Switching to median with percentile bands on the 1000-sample data eliminates this artifact.
